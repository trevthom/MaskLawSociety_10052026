Hmmm, I'm not sure I understand the question. If the company has an office in Maryland, then the SOS of Maryland should have a registered agent in Maryland for service of process in that state. The same would be true if you were working in any other state and the HQ is in Maryland. Not entirely sure about remote workers where there is no actual branch office. In any case, if you have an address for service of process, you simply need to have some proof that it was given to them. Generally a Sheriff might have a fee of $35 (pre-Biden fees) and you can mail it to him in the county where the agent is in Florida. Certified mail is another option, I recommend putting the cert number on your actual paper to eliminate any claim of them getting an "empty envelope", or you can have two witnesses certify that they watched you place the notice in the envelope and seal it and mail it, or you can ask a notary to do the same, it is called a notary presentment. Generally with that process, the notary will certify they placed your document in an envelope with postage affixed and placed it in the custody of the post office. If your notice requires a response within x days, use the notary address and they can also certify that they did or did not get a reply within the time allotted. They will then issue a certificate of non-response, or certify that whatever they sent as being the response. Fees vary for each service, If you are friends with a notary, you might be able to get it done for $20 and a glass of their favorite beverage.
