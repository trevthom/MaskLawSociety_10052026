An Illinois federal jury has awarded $425,000 in damages to former Chicago Transit Authority (CTA) employee Kevin McCormick in a case alleging religious discrimination after his termination for refusing the COVID-19 vaccine.
The jury found the CTA liable for failing to reasonably accommodate McCormick's sincerely held Catholic religious beliefs, which were based on his opposition to vaccines linked to fetal cell research.
The decision, delivered on Friday, August 29, 2025, after a four-day trial and six hours of deliberation, represents a significant outcome in a case that drew national attention to religious exemptions and workplace vaccine mandates.

The jury's verdict concluded that the CTA dismissed McCormick in April 2022 after denying his request for a religious exemption, violating Title VII of the Civil Rights Act.
McCormick's legal team argued the CTA's exemption committee applied arbitrary standards and equated his medical skepticism with insincerity of faith, failing to uphold its duty to reasonably accommodate his religion.
The CTA maintained that McCormick failed to prove a genuine religious conflict, asserting the vaccine mandate was essential for public safety during the pandemic and pointing to his prior use of medications without researching fetal cell use as undermining his claim.
The award of $425,000 is compensatory damages, and the case was initially filed seeking $2 million in damages plus back pay.
This decision follows a prior 2023 ruling by the same court that dismissed McCormick's equal protection claim but allowed his claim under the Religious Freedom Restoration Act (IRFRA) to proceed.
